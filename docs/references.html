References

Scope of This Page

This project builds directly on decades of prior published work on the Steamboat Hills geothermal field, plus several public agency datasets used throughout the pipeline. In keeping with copyright and reproducibility practice, this repository does not re-host any copyrighted source PDFs; instead, every reference below is cited so the original can be located and consulted directly, and the codebase cites these same sources inline wherever a specific number, coordinate, or method is drawn from one of them.

Steamboat Hills: Geology, Development, and Hydrothermal Structure

  • Irvin, T. and Lindsey, C.R. (2026). Renewed Measurements of Thermal Water Discharge at the Steamboat Hills Geothermal System Following Recent Fumarolic Activity. Poster, 2026 Geothermal Rising Conference. A direct continuation of Lindsey et al. (2026) below, using this project’s own integrated database to compute a chloride mass-balance estimate of thermal water inflow to Steamboat Creek and test whether the 2025 eruption reflects a new geothermal source or reactivation/redistribution within the existing conduit network. See the Results page for its key finding and the About page for a scannable link to this site.
  • Lindsey, C.R., Callahan, O., Micander, R., Hart-Wagoner, N., and Burke-Ruhl, G. (2026). Recent Geyser-Like Eruption and Renewed Hydrothermal Activity in the Steamboat Geothermal Area, Nevada. Proceedings, 51st Workshop on Geothermal Reservoir Engineering, Stanford University, SGP-TR-230. The primary account of the June 3, 2025 eruption this project’s own monitoring work is a direct continuation of: it documents the well’s history, the 2022 onset of renewed steaming ground, and the integrated water-chemistry, thermal-drone, and temperature-logging program the authors stood up in its immediate aftermath. Several of this project’s own temperature-logger placements (see the Timeline page) sit alongside sites first instrumented under that same response effort.
  • Dhakal, S., Spake, D., Lee, L., Feucht, D., Murphy, J., Akerley, J., Johnson, A., Libbey, R., and Spielman, P. (2025). Forty Years of Production from the Steamboat Geothermal Field: Numerical Model Update. Proceedings, 50th Workshop on Geothermal Reservoir Engineering, Stanford University, SGP-TR-228. The production/injection/port flow network implemented in this project’s Sampling_Ports, Production_Port_Links, and Port_Injection_Links tables is transcribed directly from this paper’s Figure 5 and Table 1.
  • Klein, C.W., Johnson, S., and Spielman, P. (2007). Exploitation at Steamboat, Nevada: What It Takes to Document and Understand the Reservoir/Groundwater/Community Interaction. Geothermal Resources Council (GRC) Transactions, Vol. 31. The primary reference for this project’s groundwater monitor-well network and the historical map of production, injection, and monitor wells.
  • Sorey, M.L. and Spielman, P. Chloride-flux estimates of geothermal fluid discharge at Steamboat Springs, presented in GRC Transactions (2008 and 2017 papers referenced in project working notes). Sorey’s chloride mass-balance method — multiplying stream chloride concentration by discharge, referenced against a deep thermal-water end-member concentration — is the historical baseline this project’s conductivity-proxy and sampling-frequency work is designed to update and extend.
  • White, D.E., Thompson, G.A., and Sandberg, C.H. (1964). Rocks, Structure, and Geologic History of Steamboat Springs Thermal Area, Washoe County, Nevada. U.S. Geological Survey Professional Paper 458-B.
  • White, D.E. (1968). Hydrology, Activity, and Heat Flow of the Steamboat Springs Thermal System, Washoe County, Nevada. U.S. Geological Survey Professional Paper 458-C. Plate 1 of this report is a geologic map showing the historical, pre-Ormat “Nevada Thermal Power” plant locations and GS-numbered exploration wells — a separate, older well-numbering scheme from the one used in current Ormat operations.
  • Cohen, P. and Loeltz, O.J. (1964). Evaluation of Hydrogeology and Hydrogeochemistry of Truckee Meadows Area, Washoe County, Nevada. U.S. Geological Survey Water-Supply Paper 1779-S.
  • Mariner, R.H. and Janik, C.J. Geochemistry of thermal waters in the Steamboat Springs area (full citation under review; referenced in project working notes as a source for historical fluid-chemistry characterization, not yet independently re-verified for this page).
  • Cohen, P. and Loeltz, O.J. (1964). Evaluation of hydrogeology and hydrochemistry of the Truckee Meadows area, Washoe County, Nevada, and a related 1964 TMWA-focused hydrogeology/hydrochemistry evaluation held in this project’s literature folder: the two documents’ relationship (same study, or a distinct companion report) has not yet been independently confirmed, since the TMWA copy is a scan with no extractable text layer.

Regional Geothermal Solute-Flux and Monitoring Methods

  • McCleskey, R.B., Clor, L.E., Lowenstern, J.B., Evans, W.C., Nordstrom, D.K., Heasler, H., and Huebner, M.A. (2012). Solute and geothermal flux monitoring using electrical conductivity in the Madison, Firehole, and Gibbon Rivers, Yellowstone National Park. Applied Geochemistry, 27(12), 2370-2381. A methodological precedent for this project’s own conductivity-as-chloride-proxy calibration work.
  • McCleskey, R.B., Lowenstern, J.B., Schaper, J., Nordstrom, D.K., Heasler, H.P., and Mahony, D. (2016). Geothermal solute flux monitoring and the source and fate of solutes in the Snake River, Yellowstone National Park, WY. Applied Geochemistry, 73, 142-156.
  • Newman, C.P. and Pepin, J.D. (2026). Prioritizing resource protection and understanding potential susceptibility of springs to surficial changes in a low-temperature geothermal system. Geothermics, 136, 103615.

Public Agency Data Sources

  • Nevada Division of Environmental Protection (NDEP). Regulatory water-quality monitoring records for Steamboat-area production and monitoring wells, obtained both through NDEP’s open-data channels and through Public Records Request PDF submittals (e.g. semi-annual digital submittals and Temporary UIC Permit compliance reporting for Permit UNEV2007204).
  • Nevada Division of Water Resources (NDWR). Well-completion (“Well Driller’s Report”) records and the statewide WellLogQuery database, used for well-location cross-referencing and construction detail throughout this project’s well-log ingestion pipeline.
  • Nevada Bureau of Mines and Geology (NBMG). Geothermal_Wells dataset, ArcGIS Open Data, data-nbmg.opendata.arcgis.com/datasets/72341ba987e34c12a575c83f1d7c5367_0. The primary source for well coordinates and construction metadata used to resolve well identities across this project, matched by exact well name against a Washoe County / Steamboat-area bounding box.
  • U.S. Geological Survey (USGS). Live discharge, historic grab-sample specific conductance, and water-quality data for Steamboat Creek and surrounding stations, accessed via the National Water Information System and Water Quality Portal.
  • National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). Regional precipitation and temperature records used as environmental context alongside the hydrothermal monitoring record.
  • Nevada Division of Environmental Protection, Bureau of Safe Drinking Water (2024). State of Nevada Public Water Systems Annual Compliance Report. Regulatory context for Steamboat-area public water systems.
  • Nevada Division of Environmental Protection (2020). Hidden Valley-Steamboat Creek, Steamboat Valley-Steamboat Creek, and Thomas Creek HUC-12 Watershed Profiles, from the 2020 Integrated Source Water and 319(h) Watershed Protection Plan. Watershed-scale water-quality context for the drainages surrounding the Steamboat field.
  • Nevada Division of Water Resources / State Engineer. Steamboat Hot Springs permitted-well index map, Township 18N Range 20E. A State Engineer well-permit reference map used alongside the WellLogQuery database for well-location cross-referencing.

Methods and Software

  • Parkhurst, D.L. and Appelo, C.A.J. PHREEQC — geochemical speciation, saturation-index, and inverse-modeling software, U.S. Geological Survey. The intended engine for this project’s forward and inverse geochemical modeling once its input-table pipeline is fully wired in.
  • R Core Team and the tidyverse, DBI/RSQLite, sf, leaflet, and rmarkdown package ecosystems. The full data-engineering, spatial-analysis, and web-publishing stack this platform runs on.

A Note on Citation Practice

Where this project’s own code, database notes, or documentation draw a specific number, coordinate, or method from one of the sources above, the citation is recorded inline at the point of use (in script comments, in AGENTS.md provenance notes, or in the relevant page of this website) rather than only listed here in aggregate. If you use this platform’s data or outputs in your own work, please cite both this project (see the About page) and the original source of any specific published result you rely on.
