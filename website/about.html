About This Project

This project was developed as part of a graduate-level data, geochemistry, and GIS workflow at the University of Nevada, Reno, within the Great Basin Center for Geothermal Energy (GBCGE).

🔗 Repository:
https://github.com/tylerirvin543/Steamboat_Creek_Geochemistry_Database

🌋 Research Context

The Steamboat Springs geothermal field represents a long-studied hydrothermal system undergoing renewed activity following decades of geothermal production.

After the decline of surface expressions during sustained fluid extraction, the system reactivated in June 2025 with a geyser-like eruption exceeding 30 feet from an abandoned well.

This reactivation raises fundamental questions about:

  • subsurface pressure dynamics
  • fluid pathways and connectivity
  • geochemical evolution of the system

🔬 Research Questions

This workflow is designed to support investigation of several key hydrothermal questions:

System Source & Driving Processes

  • Is the system magmatic or amagmatic in origin?
  • What role does deep fluid input play in controlling chemistry and temperature?

Hydrologic Connectivity

  • Is the Lower Sinter Terrace hydrothermally connected to the main reservoir?
  • If connected, to what degree and through what pathways?

Fluid Evolution & Chemistry

  • How does chemistry evolve during transport and discharge?
  • Are we observing:
    • concentration of solutes through boiling
    • CO₂ degassing
    • pH increase from buffering processes?

Temporal Variability

  • How do temperature and chemistry evolve through time?
  • Can short-term signals reveal subsurface flow or pressure changes?

💧 Hydrology and Flux-Based Methods

A key component of this research is the use of chloride flux (Cl‑flux) and related hydrologic methods to estimate mass and energy transport through the system.

These approaches allow us to:

  • estimate total hydrothermal discharge
  • quantify solute transport
  • constrain subsurface flow rates
  • link chemistry with hydrologic behavior

Flux estimates are integrated with:

  • field transect measurements
  • spatial analysis in ArcGIS
  • database-driven aggregation workflows

🧪 From Data to Geochemical Modeling

The ultimate goal of this system is to support quantitative geochemical interpretation.

This includes:

Mass Balance and Inverse Modeling

  • reconstructing fluid mixing processes
  • identifying end-member compositions
  • quantifying gains/losses during flow

PHREEQC Integration

Validated chemistry is transformed into PHREEQC-ready inputs to enable:

  • forward speciation modeling
  • inverse modeling of hydrothermal processes
  • evaluation of mineral-fluid equilibria

This creates a direct chain from:

field observations → database → validated chemistry → geochemical models → interpretation

🗺️ Spatial Analysis & GIS Integration

All data are structured to support ArcGIS-based spatial analysis via GeoPackage exports.

This enables:

  • querying hydrothermal features spatially
  • mapping temperature and chemistry distributions
  • integrating Cl‑flux transects and discharge estimates
  • linking spatial patterns to subsurface processes

🧠 Why This Workflow Matters

Hydrothermal systems are inherently complex, and traditional approaches often suffer from:

  • disconnected datasets
  • lack of temporal resolution
  • limited reproducibility

This system addresses those limitations by providing:

  • a single source of truth (relational database)
  • reproducible ingestion and transformation workflows
  • integration across chemistry, temperature, and spatial data
  • a framework for iterative hypothesis testing

🧰 Tools and Technologies

This project integrates multiple tools across data engineering, GIS, and geochemical modeling:

  • R (tidyverse, DBI, readxl, ggplot2)
  • SQLite (relational database)
  • ArcGIS / GeoPackage (spatial analysis)
  • PHREEQC (geochemical modeling)

🚀 Project Scope and Impact

While focused on Steamboat Springs, this workflow is designed to be transferable to:

  • geothermal monitoring systems
  • hydrothermal research studies
  • environmental and geochemical datasets
  • long-term observational networks

✅ Summary

This project is not just a database — it is a reproducible scientific framework for understanding hydrothermal systems through time.

It connects:

  • field data
  • regulatory datasets
  • sensor measurements
  • spatial analysis
  • geochemical modeling

into a unified, extensible system for research and discovery.
